# Tabla de composición de alimentos para bovinos de carne — trópico sudamericano (Santa Cruz, Bolivia)

**Feed composition table for beef cattle rations — tropical South America**
Compiled 10 September 2026. All values are on a **dry-matter (DM) basis** except DM itself, which is % as-fed.

## How to read this table

| Convention | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `n.a.` | Not available / not confirmed in any page fetched for this compilation (or not applicable, e.g. starch in molasses). |
| `≈ x ᵉ` | **Estimated TDN**, not a published TDN. Calculated from the digestible energy (DE, MJ/kg DM) published on the cited Feedipedia datasheet, using the standard equivalence 1 % TDN = 0.04409 Mcal DE/kg DM (i.e. TDN % = DE MJ/kg ÷ 4.184 ÷ 0.04409). For high-fat feeds this convention can exceed 100. Where a source publishes TDN directly, the plain number is given and the source is cited. |
| RUP | Where taken from Feedipedia, RUP = 100 − effective ruminal N degradability at k = 6 %/h (the standard beef passage rate); the degradability figure published on the datasheet is subtracted from 100. These are *in situ* degradability estimates, not NASEM RUP coefficients, and for oilseeds they can run lower than the RUP values used in North American beef systems (e.g. whole cottonseed 11 % here, from a published effective degradability of 89 %). Treat them as indicative and prefer a published RUP if your engine already carries one. |
| Minerals | Feedipedia publishes macro-minerals as g/kg DM; they have been divided by 10 to give % DM. No other transformation was applied. |
| ⚠ temperate | The row is a temperate analogue, not the tropical cultivar requested. Flagged explicitly. |

Feedipedia (INRAE / CIRAD / AFZ / FAO) datasheet values are **means of large multi-study datasets** (often hundreds to thousands of samples) and are the backbone of this table because they are the only source that reports the full nutrient panel consistently for tropical feeds. Cultivar-specific rows (Mombaça, Tanzânia, Zuri, Marandu, Piatã, Maralfalfa, King grass, Tifton 85) come from peer-reviewed or institutional publications and therefore describe **one site, one season, one fertilisation level** — treat them as indicative and prefer the species-level Feedipedia row when formulating unless you have local analyses.

## 8. Divergent values between sources, and which source to trust

The rows below are the feeds where the sources fetched in this session disagreed enough to change a ration.

**1. Tropical grass CP at "mid-vegetative" stage — 4 to 16 % DM depending on the source.**
Feedipedia's species means (Guinea 11.2 %, brizantha 10.4 %, decumbens 8.9 %) sit near the middle; Oklahoma State reports bermuda pasture "vegetative" at **16 % CP** and Feedipedia bermuda fresh at **9.8 %** ([OSU ANSI‑3018](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/print-publications/ansi/nutritive-values-of-feeds-ansi-3018-a.pdf); [Feedipedia](https://www.feedipedia.org/node/11847)); the UFMS Mombaça trial gives only **6.5 % at 49 d** ([UFMS](https://posgraduacao.ufms.br/portal/trabalho-arquivos/download/8155)) while the Marandu N trial reaches **17.7 % at 200 kg N/ha** ([Ciência e Agrotecnologia](https://www.scielo.br/j/cagro/a/V5cgL54jnBW3BFgwnthwHdL/)). *Most reliable:* the Feedipedia species means for unfertilised or lightly fertilised Santa Cruz pastures, because they are multi-hundred-sample tropical datasets; the US extension values are from fertilised temperate/subtropical swards and overestimate CP for Bolivian conditions. If the pasture is N-fertilised, shift CP upward toward the Marandu trial values.

**2. Mombaça NDF/ADF — 73.1/47.7 % at 49 d (UFMS) vs 73.6/43.9 % at 28-d cutting (Embrapa) vs 79.1/60.3 % at 65 d (UFMS pre-ensiling).**
([UFMS](https://posgraduacao.ufms.br/portal/trabalho-arquivos/download/8155); [Freitas et al., Embrapa](https://www.alice.cnptia.embrapa.br/alice/bitstream/doc/209824/1/BJv23n3Freitas.pdf)) *Most reliable for a grazing ration:* the Embrapa 28-day-interval values, because they match how Mombaça is actually rotationally grazed and because that paper also reports the full macro-mineral panel measured on the same samples.

**3. Maralfalfa — CP 21.8 % (56 d) vs 18.7 % (65 d after establishment) vs 11.9 % (105 d).**
([LRRD 18(6)](https://lrrd.cipav.org.co/lrrd18/6/corr18084.htm); [Agrodivulgación COLPOS](https://agrodivulgacion-colpos.org/index.php/1agrodivulgacion1/article/download/77/241/1237)) *Most reliable:* the LRRD paper, because it reports both ages with the full panel (NDF, lignin, EE, ash, TDN, Ca, P, K, Mg) from the same samples and states the analytical methods. Note that the commonly quoted "Maralfalfa 16–18 % CP" figures collapse to ~12 % once the stand is past 100 days.

**4. King grass NDF — 86.2 % at 45 d but 72.1 % at 60 d and 80.4 % at 90 d in the same trial.**
([UPSE, Santa Elena](https://repositorio.upse.edu.ec/server/api/core/bitstreams/ed23d5ad-b28e-4e28-aa2c-32e0dbed18b7/content)) The 45-day figure is non-monotonic with the rest of the series and is almost certainly an analytical or transcription problem. *Most reliable:* the 60-day row (NDF 72.1, ADF 47.6), which is also consistent with Feedipedia's elephant grass mean of NDF 71.5 / ADF 42.5 ([Feedipedia](https://www.feedipedia.org/node/12365)).

**5. Alfalfa hay TDN — ≈58 % (from Feedipedia DE) vs 60 % published (OSU, midbloom) vs 63 % (OSU, early bloom).**
([Feedipedia](https://www.feedipedia.org/node/11743); [OSU ANSI‑3018](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/print-publications/ansi/nutritive-values-of-feeds-ansi-3018-a.pdf)) *Most reliable:* the OSU published TDN matched to the visual maturity of the lot, because it is a directly measured beef-cattle TDN rather than an energy conversion; the agreement within 2–5 units is reassuring for the ᵉ-estimated TDN used elsewhere in this table.

**6. Corn silage — NDF 44.3 % and starch 29.1 % (Feedipedia, DM 30–35 %) vs NDF 36.9 % and starch 34.1 % (commercial-lab median, DM 35.4 %).**
([Feedipedia](https://www.feedipedia.org/node/12871); [Dairyland Laboratories](https://dairylandlabs.com/hubfs/Corn%20silage-1.pdf?hsLang=en)) *Most reliable:* for modern high-grain hybrids harvested at 33–38 % DM, the Dairyland median (n > 300,000 samples) is the better default; the Feedipedia mean is the better default for the lower-grain, hand-harvested silages still common in Santa Cruz. This is exactly the "normal vs high-grain" split requested, and it is why both rows are kept.

**7. Sugarcane silage — TDN 56.6 % and CP 1.9 % (no additive) vs fresh cane before ensiling TDN 63.4 % and CP 1.4 %.**
([Avaliação de silagens de cana‑de‑açúcar](https://scispace.com/pdf/avaliacao-de-silagens-de-cana-de-acucar-in-natura-e-com-2yuibmqgz2.pdf)) Ensiling untreated cane cost ~7 TDN points and raised NDF from 49.0 to 65.2 %. *Most reliable:* both, as a pair — they quantify the ethanol/sugar loss of additive-free cane silage and justify budgeting cane silage at ~56 TDN rather than at the fresh-cane value.

**8. Dicalcium phosphate — Ca 23.9 %/P 17.0 % (INRAE‑AFZ) vs Ca 20.0 %/P 18.5 % (NDSU).**
([INRAE‑CIRAD‑AFZ](https://www.feedtables.com/content/dicalcium-phosphate-dihydrate); [NDSU AS1287](https://www.library.nd.gov/statedocs/NDSUExtensionService/as128720100324.pdf)) *Most reliable:* the INRAE‑AFZ values for a European/South American dihydrate product (n = 599 for Ca, n = 660 for P, with ranges), while the NDSU figures better describe the anhydrous/blended DCP sold in North America. The practical rule is to formulate against the supplier's own certificate of analysis, because the Ca:P ratio moves from 1.29 to 1.08 between these two.

**9. Grass "TDN" in general.** Where this table shows `≈ x ᵉ`, the number is a conversion of Feedipedia DE and not a measured TDN. Cross-checks where both exist are close (Marandu 54.6–56.7 published vs 55 ᵉ for the species; Piatã 60.6 published vs 55 ᵉ), but the ᵉ values run high for fats and oilseeds (full-fat soybeans 108 ᵉ, peanut meal 100 ᵉ). *Recommendation for the formulation engine:* use the ᵉ values for forages, and for fat-rich concentrates substitute a published NASEM/BR‑CORTE TDN instead of the ᵉ figure.

## 9. Coverage notes and gaps

- **No confirmed data found in this session** for: *Megathyrsus maximus* cv. **Zuri** minerals, EE, ash and TDN; **Flemingia macrophylla** minerals, EE, lignin and TDN; **pasto llanero** as a distinct cultivar (*B. dictyoneura* / *B. humidicola* cv. Llanero) — the *B. humidicola* species row is used instead; a pure **Maralfalfa silage** panel; **King grass silage** as such; sulfur for most Feedipedia rows (Feedipedia rarely reports S); starch for forages (rarely measured, and not applicable for most tropical grasses).
- **Temperate analogues used and flagged:** ryegrass hay, tall fescue hay, and the US extension rows for alfalfa hay, oat hay, bermuda pasture, corn/sorghum/oat silage.
- **Structural caution:** Feedipedia mineral means come from worldwide datasets; soil-driven minerals (P, S, Ca, K) in Santa Cruz forages can differ by a factor of two from these means. Before the ration engine is used commercially, at minimum P, Ca, S and K of the local pastures and of the cane/silage should be measured locally; the energy and fibre columns are far more transferable than the mineral columns.
